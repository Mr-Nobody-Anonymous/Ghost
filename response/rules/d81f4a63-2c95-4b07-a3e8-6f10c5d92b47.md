# NXDOMAIN Response for a Long High-Entropy Label

- Rule: `d81f4a63-2c95-4b07-a3e8-6f10c5d92b47`
- Severity: low
- ATT&CK: T1568.002
- Tactic: command-and-control
- Status: **drafted from the rule, not worked against a live alert**

## What fired

Member rule of the DGA NXDOMAIN burst correlation. Flags a single NXDOMAIN answer whose first label is at least 14 lowercase characters long - the label shape produced by the domain generator, distinguished from normal CDN names by the NXDOMAIN result. A bare NXDOMAIN is not a finding; the correlation is.

Event IDs in scope: none.

## Triage

Check these in order. The first one that is empty means the rule matched on a field the collector did not populate.

- `rcode`
- `Source`
- `query`

Compare the destination against the process that resolved it. A browser is expected to resolve unfamiliar names; an Office process is not.

## Containment

`block-domain` - Block the destination at the resolver and the proxy. Record the full URI and every header the proxy retained before the block goes in.

Do not close out on the containment action alone. The entry being removed does not answer how it got there.

## What to preserve

- The alert's raw event, verbatim, before any field is normalised
- The host's clock offset from the collector
- The rule version that fired, from the rule's own id

## When this is a false positive

The behaviour this rule matches has legitimate causes on a managed host. Before closing, identify the parent process and whether the binary is signed; a signed path under `Program Files` with a service-account parent is the ordinary case.
